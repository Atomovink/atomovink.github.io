Research · EXP-001

Why does AI-discovered stability collapse on more complete reference hulls?

Decomposing and predicting reference-hull sensitivity of generative crystal models.

In progress Started 3 Nov 2026 Generative AI · MLIPs · Thermodynamic stability

The observation

Generative models propose new crystals, and their success is usually measured by how many are stable, unique and novel (SUN). Stability is judged against a convex hull built from a reference database. The same samples score very differently depending on that reference:

DiffCSP3.8%0.2%
PLaID++12.0%1.0%

Source: LeMat-GenBench (arXiv 2512.04562), UMA potential, pre-relaxation tables. MatterGen's own paper reports a similar effect: 13% below the hull against Materials Project versus 3% against Alex-MP-ICSD.

The gap

The drop has been reported but not explained. We do not know how much of it comes from lost novelty (the "new" material is already in the larger database) versus displaced stability (a newly added phase lowers the hull), which chemistries are most affected, or whether fragile claims can be spotted in advance.

Related work studies a different axis: chemistry-resolved disagreement between PBE(+U) and r2SCAN stability verdicts in GNoME (arXiv 2610.33101). LeMat-GenBench already covers ensembles of potentials and self-consistent hulls.

Research question

Why do generative models' stability and novelty rates collapse on more complete reference hulls, and can we predict which "stable" claims are fragile?

Method

  1. Candidates: public samples from MatterGen, DiffCSP and other generators, plus a control set of known structures.
  2. Nested references built from LeMat-Bulk by source: H1 = Materials Project, H2 = H1 + Alexandria, H3 = H2 + OQMD.
  3. Self-consistent hulls: the same ML potential scores candidates and reference phases (released MACE energies for LeMat-Bulk), with DFT hulls as a secondary check.
  4. Decomposition: every lost SUN label is classed as lost novelty, displaced stability (with the responsible phase recorded), or both.
  5. Fragility analysis by number of elements, reference-phase density, element groups and generator.
  6. Hull-confidence score: a simple, interpretable predictor of whether a stability claim survives more reference data, tested on held-out chemical systems.

Safeguards

  • ML potentials are not DFT: results say "predicted stable".
  • Even the largest reference is incomplete, so survival rates are upper bounds.
  • Energy references are kept consistent and never silently mixed.
  • Thermodynamic stability is not synthesizability.
  • Every source ID, database version and model version is recorded.

Outputs

  • Open code and a frozen, versioned set of nested reference hulls
  • A dataset of every candidate's labels under each hull
  • The hull-confidence score as a reusable tool (the basis of Atomovink Verify)
  • A technical report, and a preprint if the results hold
